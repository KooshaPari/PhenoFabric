# User-Facing Differentiators and Product Requirements

## The product must feel simpler than its architecture

The graph canvas is the expert surface, not the mandatory daily surface. Ordinary use centers on workspaces, search, drag-to-display, focus cycling and route health.

### Differentiators

1. **Seat follows the person.** A workspace can move from desk to couch without moving processes unless doing so is useful.
2. **Any sink can be a monitor, portal, mirror or takeover.** The same MacBook panel can display a remote monitor, one application, a pinned test surface or its native desktop.
3. **Input and output focus are independent but transactionally coupled when requested.** No blind interval and no stuck modifiers.
4. **Quality policy matches content.** Desktop text prefers 4:4:4; games prefer frame pacing; HDR content preserves transfer/gamut metadata; color work chooses controlled transforms.
5. **Foreground is protected.** Users see whether Ableton/game guarantees are admitted, threatened or degraded before failure.
6. **Agents publish, not hijack.** New VMs and reports appear in a surface catalog with TTL and purpose.
7. **One failure does not strand the machine.** Every important host has a fallback console and an emergency local input path.
8. **The system explains itself.** “Why this route?”, “why this node?”, “what moved?”, “what was copied?”, and “what would improve it?” are product actions.

## Packaging bar

- one installer per OS and one account/device-enrollment flow;
- local-first operation without a mandatory hosted coordinator;
- guided driver/permission setup with explicit security boundaries;
- workspace templates for desk, couch, Ableton, gaming, bench and remote modes;
- auto-discovery that never auto-grants control;
- adapter diagnostics and deterministic fallback;
- no requirement that users understand KVMFR, RAIL, PipeWire, QUIC or RDMA to use the product.
