# Defensibility, Moat, and Competitive Risk

## Plausible moat

The defensible asset is not a codec or one driver. It is the accumulated capability model, route compiler, measured cost data, adaptive region policies, cross-platform adapter quality, verification corpus and ecosystem integration.

- topology and capability fingerprints across real hardware;
- stage-level latency/copy/quality models;
- policies that preserve game/audio deadlines under mixed load;
- object-residency and workload-placement history;
- normalized app/window/input/audio semantics across OSes;
- robust fallback behavior and fault corpus;
- human/agent intent-to-route explainability;
- integration with AgilePlus requirements and Tracera evidence.

## Risks that destroy the moat

1. **Lowest-common-denominator implementation:** always encode pixels and RPC everything.
2. **Demo-only performance:** works on idle hosts but collapses under compiler/game/agent load.
3. **Opaque optimization:** no evidence that selected routes beat alternatives.
4. **Driver sprawl:** privileged platform code grows faster than it can be secured and signed.
5. **Vendor lock-in:** one hosted transport or GPU vendor becomes mandatory.
6. **Graph UI fetish:** powerful diagram but poor simple workflows.
7. **Universal-OS overclaim:** arbitrary binaries cannot safely be split at every instruction across OSes.
8. **No adoption wedge:** all features require the final distributed runtime before delivering utility.

## Counter-positioning

Ship useful shells and adapters first: workspaces, seat switching, Looking Glass orchestration, MacBook display sink, open LAN streaming, agent surface publishing and foreground QoS. The research fabric grows behind a product already valuable on one host and two devices.
